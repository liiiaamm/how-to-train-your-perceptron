How to train your perceptron

In 1958 Frank Rosenblatt wired 400 photocells to a bank of motor-driven knobs and called it the Mark I Perceptron. It learned by turning those knobs. This page lets you turn them yourself, then shows the one problem a single neuron can never solve, and the fix.

Start with one neuron

One neuron, two inputs

A perceptron adds up its inputs, each multiplied by a weight, plus a bias:

\[ u = b + w_1 x_1 + w_2 x_2 \]

Then it squashes that number into the range 0 to 1 with a sigmoid, \( F = \sigma(u) = 1/(1+e^{-u}) \), and says “yes” when \(F\) passes a threshold \(\tau\). Every point where \(F = \tau\) lies on a straight line. That line is the whole decision.

Drag the two round handles to move the line, or use the faders. The background shows how confident the neuron is: deeper colour means further from the threshold.

Learning is walking downhill

Nobody sets the knobs by hand. We measure how wrong the neuron is with the squared error over the four examples,

\[ L(b, w_1, w_2) = \sum_{k=1}^{4} \bigl(y_k - F(x^{(k)})\bigr)^2 , \]

and nudge every weight a little in the direction that lowers \(L\). Because \(\sigma'(u) = \sigma(u)(1-\sigma(u))\), each nudge is cheap to compute. Press train and watch the line find its place from a random start.

The XOR wall

Exclusive-or says yes when exactly one input is on. Put the four answers on the plane and the two blue points sit on opposite corners. Try to separate them with one line.

XOR, one neuron 2 of 4 correct

2best you’ve managed, out of 4

Three is the ceiling. No straight line puts both blue corners on one side and both yellow corners on the other, so no amount of training can get a single neuron to four. Gradient descent gives up in the middle, with F close to 0.5 everywhere.

Bend the plane

The fix is to stop looking at the original plane. Add a hidden layer of two neurons. Each one draws its own line, and together they map every point \(x\) to a new point

\[ z = \bigl(\sigma(W^{[1]}x + b^{[1]})_1,\; \sigma(W^{[1]}x + b^{[1]})_2\bigr) \]

An output neuron then draws one straight line in \(z\)-space. Train the network, then drag the slider to watch the plane bend. Where the grid ends up, the two blue points have been pulled to the same side.

Two inputs, two hidden neurons, one output

Press train. About two seconds of gradient descent, nine weights.
